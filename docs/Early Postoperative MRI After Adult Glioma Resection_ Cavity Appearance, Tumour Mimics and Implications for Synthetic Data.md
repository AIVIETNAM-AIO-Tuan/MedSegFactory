# Early postoperative MRI after adult glioma resection: what the cavity and its surroundings usually look like (≤72 h)

In the first 72 h after adult glioma resection, the typical scan shows a fluid-filled cavity with variable blood, air and haemostatic material. Benign thin linear enhancement at the cavity rim is common and becomes more frequent with every hour after about 36 h. Restricted diffusion at the cavity margin is seen in roughly a third to two-thirds of patients. In one GBM series, 43% of these infarcted foci enhanced on follow-up (Ulmer 2006). Intrinsic T1 hyperintensity from blood is the most common mimic of residual enhancing tumour (rET), and only pre-contrast T1 (ideally with subtraction) separates it.

## TL;DR
- **What is common (≤72 h), all in adult glioma unless stated:**
  - Benign thin linear rim enhancement: 50.8% of GBM scans before 48 h and 69.1% after (Rykkje 2023, n=264, mostly 1.5 T).
  - Scans with no enhancement at all: fall from 36.7% at 0–36 h to 4.5% at ≥60 h (same study).
  - DWI restriction at the cavity margin: 31–70% (pooled 48% in a 29-study meta-analysis).
  - Intradural blood ≥0.1 mL: 63% of 292 resections at 12–48 h.
  - Early T1-hyperintense methaemoglobin: 44% (24/54).
  - Air and extra-axial fluid: near-universal in epilepsy-surgery series.
- **What separates mimics from rET:**
  - Blood products: pre-contrast T1, ideally a T1ce−T1 subtraction, plus SWI/T2*.
  - Ischaemia: DWI/ADC.
  - Haemostats: T2 and GRE/SWI.
  - Benign enhancement: thin (<3 mm), linear, along the cavity wall or dura, and not at a site of preoperative nodular enhancement.
  - BraTS 2024 explicitly excludes thin linear enhancement from the "enhancing tissue" label and gives annotators a subtraction image.
- **What is unknown:**
  - No study found reports cavity signal intensity relative to WM/GM/CSF, or ADC of cavity contents.
  - No study gives the frequency of FLAIR-hyperintense, non-enhancing cavities. Your pilot "T1-not-dark, FLAIR-bright" minority is most plausibly blood-tinged or proteinaceous fluid or clot, but that is my inference, not a measured finding.
  - No study measured the effect of 5-ALA, irrigation, ventricular opening or haemostat choice on cavity appearance.

## 1. One-page summary of the common early findings

**Cavity contents (≤72 h)**
- **Fluid:**
  - **Typical cavity:** CSF-like fluid, dark on T1 and bright on T2.
  - **Variable cavities:** BraTS 2024 annotators were told that recent cavities "often contained air, blood, and/or proteinaceous materials, exhibiting variable signal characteristics". In contrast, chronic cavities (older than 3–6 months) are CSF-isointense on T1 and T2/FLAIR.\[1\]
- **Blood:**
  - **Volumetric glioma series:** 184/292 resections (63%) showed ≥0.1 mL intradural blood and 212/292 (73%) extradural blood at 12–48 h (Ølstad 2024). Blood was defined as iso-to-hyperintense on T1 with T2 hypointensity.\[2\]
  - **Early methaemoglobin:** a Heidelberg cohort showed early T1 hyperintensity in 24/54 (44%) glioblastoma patients (Meyding-Lamadé 1993). The authors attributed the unusually early methaemoglobin to hydrogen peroxide used for haemostasis.\[3\]\[4\]
  - **Serial study:** methaemoglobin appeared at some time point in 17/17 (100%) malignant-glioma patients scanned serially and interfered with residual-tumour assessment in 6/17 (35%) (Forsyth 1997).\[5\]
- **Air:** 31/35 (89%) of images in the first 4 days showed intracranial air, and none after day 5. This is a non-tumour series (epilepsy temporal lobectomy; Sato 1997).\[2\]
- **Extra-axial fluid:** seen on all 46 images in the first 30 days and gone by day 51 (Sato 1997, epilepsy). Henegar 1996 found extra-axial fluid, air or blood in 11/11 epilepsy patients at 17–28 h.\[2\]

**Enhancement**
- **Rim enhancement:**
  - **GBM series:** thin linear rim enhancement in 93/183 (50.8%) of scans before 48 h and 56/81 (69.1%) at ≥48 h (Rykkje 2023, 90.4% at 1.5 T).\[6\]
  - **Benign fraction:** in Bette 2016 (173 GBM, 3 T), linear enhancement was 24.1% before 45 h and 45.5% after. Of these, 61.5% and 75% respectively were judged surgically induced on follow-up.\[7\]
  - **Earliest onset:** benign enhancement has been documented from 17 h in non-tumour cortex (Henegar 1996) and 22 h 57 min after brain-tumour surgery (Lescher 2014, 3 T).\[8\]\[9\]\[10\]
- **Dural and leptomeningeal enhancement:**
  - **Epilepsy surgery:** dural enhancement on 96/97 (99%) of postoperative images (Sato 1997). Henegar found it adjacent to the craniotomy in 11/11 and remote in 73%; pia-arachnoid enhancement over the ipsilateral convexity occurred in 8/11.\[2\]\[8\]

**Surroundings**
- **Ischaemia:**
  - **Rates on DWI:** 26/84 (31%) newly diagnosed gliomas (Gempt 2013, J Neurosurg 118:801); 35/50 (70%) GBM (Ulmer 2006); 28/44 (64%) newly diagnosed gliomas (Smith 2005); 238/539 (44%) diffuse glioma operations (320 primary operations, 219 reoperations) in a population-based cohort (Strand et al., Acta Neurochir 2021;163:3097–3108).
  - **Later enhancement:** Ulmer reported enhancement of the infarcted tissue in 43% on follow-up. Smith reported enhancement from day 15 to day 198.\[11\]\[12\]
- **Oedema/T2-FLAIR:** pre-existing peritumoural oedema persists, and new surgically induced oedema can be added. No ≤72 h frequency with denominators was found.

**Timing in one line:** each additional hour after about 36 h adds benign enhancement. ≤48 h is the guideline window (EANO 2021, RANO-era practice), and Rykkje's data show the cleanest scans at ≤36 h.\[6\]

## 4. Mimics of residual enhancing tumour and how to tell them apart

1. **Intrinsic T1 hyperintensity (methaemoglobin, clot, proteinaceous fluid).**
   - **How common:** the most common mimic; 44% early, interfering in 35% (Forsyth).\[5\]
   - **Discriminator:** bright on pre-contrast T1 at the same location.
   - **Practice:** BraTS 2024 gave annotators a T1-Gd − T1 subtraction "to distinguish areas of intrinsic T1 hyperintensity from enhancement". BraTS lists "segmentation of non-enhancing lesions that have intrinsic T1 hyperintensity as ET" among the most typical automated errors.\[1\] The 956-patient early-postoperative GBM segmentation dataset (Helland/Bouget et al., arXiv 2304.08881) defined residual tumour as "enhancing tissue in the T1w-CE scan, but darker in the T1w scan".\[28\]
   - **Adjuncts:** SWI/T2* blooming supports blood. Forsting 1993 states that with pre- and post-contrast imaging "it is usually easy to differentiate T1-shortening due to residual enhancing tumor from early methemoglobin formation."\[3\]\[29\]
   - **Sequences needed:** T1 + T1ce (± subtraction), SWI/T2*.
2. **Benign thin linear rim enhancement.**
   - **Discriminators:** <3 mm, linear and smooth, follows the cavity wall, and not at a site of preoperative nodular enhancement. Thick, nodular or "mass-like enhancement in areas showing tumor on the preoperative scans" suggests tumour (RANO-derived trial protocol text).\[26\]
   - **Sequences needed:** T1ce, T1 and the preoperative T1ce.
   - **Caveat:** in Lescher, 6/15 (40%) of patients with reactive enhancement also had residual tumour elsewhere in the cavity.\[20\] In Rykkje, nodular enhancement occurred in 28–30% of patients without measurable tumour, and radiologists were "uncertain" about non-measurable residual in 23–33%.\[6\]
3. **Dural/leptomeningeal enhancement.**
   - **Discriminators:** smooth and extra-axial, near the craniotomy or at intergyral/interlobar interfaces (Lescher), and may be remote.\[20\]
   - **Sequences needed:** T1ce, read for location.
4. **Ischaemia/infarct.**
   - **At ≤72 h:** does not enhance but is DWI-bright and ADC-low.
   - **Later:** in Ulmer 2006, 43% of infarcted foci enhanced on follow-up. This matters mainly for scans beyond 72 h and for any generator that simulates later time points.
   - **Sequences needed:** DWI/ADC.
5. **Haemostatic materials.**
   - **Appearance:** gelatin-thrombin matrix shows T2-dark speckles and GRE dark signal. Oxidised cellulose is T2-dark early.\[16\]\[17\]\[18\]
   - **Intraoperative false positives:** on intraoperative MRI, false-positive "residual tumour" occurred in 4/124 HGG cases (3.2%): 2 haematomas, 1 haemostatic agent, 1 contrast pooling in a dependent part of the cavity (SNO abstract NIMG-73, PMC11553993).\[30\]
   - **Sequences needed:** T2, GRE/SWI.
6. **Vessels and choroid plexus.**
   - **Why they matter:** the first common error listed by BraTS 2024.\[1\]
   - **Discriminator:** tubular morphology and continuity across slices.
   - **Sequences needed:** T1ce (3D).
7. **Contrast leakage into the cavity after intraoperative gadolinium (iMRI or 5-ALA-era workflows).**
   - **Evidence:** new iMRI enhancement in 16% at 3 T, which decreased or resolved by early postoperative MRI (Miskin).\[22\]\[23\] A J Neurosurg comparison ("Comparative analysis of intraoperative MRI and early postoperative MRI findings in glioma surgery patients", J Neurosurg 142(5):1289, published December 2024; 124 cases, 20 non-gliomas excluded) reported that "the incidence of enhancement at the resection cavity was 20.8% with iMRI and 47.8% with epMRI, which may mimic residual tumor". On iMRI, cavity enhancement accounted for 5 of 24 surgically induced enhancements. The full text was blocked, so authors remain unverified.

## 6. Implications for a synthetic early-postoperative MRI generator

**Recommendations** (my inferences, grounded in the frequencies above):

1. **Include by default (common at ≤72 h):**
   - Mostly CSF-like cavity fluid.
   - Thin linear rim enhancement in about 40–75% of non-residual cases. Scale it with a "hours since surgery" parameter: about 40% at ≤36 h, about 60% at 37–59 h, about 75% at ≥60 h.
   - Smooth dural enhancement under the craniotomy, which is near-universal in non-tumour data.
   - Small intracavitary or marginal blood (T1 iso-to-bright, T2 dark, SWI blooming) in roughly 60% of cases.
   - Pockets of air (signal void) and extra-axial fluid/air.
   - Pre-existing FLAIR oedema.
   - DWI-bright rim or sector restriction in roughly 30–50%, if you synthesise DWI.
2. **Include as a minority variant:**
   - A cavity that is not dark on T1 and is bright on FLAIR, without enhancement. It is consistent with your pilot. Physically it is explained by blood- or protein-containing fluid failing FLAIR nulling (Melhem phantom thresholds; BraTS "variable signal"). No published frequency exists, so calibrate it to your own real data.
   - Haemostat-like T2-dark speckles in a minority of cases.
   - Early T1-bright methaemoglobin rims (44% in one older cohort).
3. **Findings that need sequences other than T1ce to be classified correctly:**
   - T1-bright blood vs enhancement: pre-contrast T1 or subtraction, plus SWI.
   - Ischaemia vs tumour: DWI/ADC (non-enhancing at ≤72 h, so mainly relevant for label consistency).
   - Haemostats: T2/SWI.
   - Proteinaceous vs CSF fluid: FLAIR.
   - Any generator that outputs T1ce without a coherent pre-contrast T1 will teach the segmenter nothing about the most common mimic. Generate T1 and T1ce jointly, so that blood is bright on both and enhancement is bright only on T1ce.
4. **Labels:**
   - **rET:** only thick or nodular enhancement that is darker on T1 and located at or adjacent to a site of preoperative enhancing tumour, consistent with BraTS ET, Helland/Bouget, and RANO "nodular or mass-like".
   - **Not rET:** thin linear rim, dural and pial enhancement, vessels and choroid plexus, T1-bright blood, haemostats, air, DWI-only ischaemia.
   - **Edge cases:** thick linear and diffuse enhancement are genuinely ambiguous in the literature. Consider an "uncertain" class or exclusion, instead of forcing a label.
5. **Scenario control:** add "hours since surgery" (≤36, 37–59, ≥60 h) and "field strength" as generator inputs. Timing measurably changes benign enhancement prevalence. The effect of field strength is plausible but unquantified.

## 7. Determinants: tumour vs surgery vs timing

- **Timing (best evidence):**
  - **Enhancement:** strong and dose-like effect on benign enhancement (Rykkje 2023, Bette 2016, Lescher 2014, Smets 2013, Forsyth 1997).
  - **Resection status:** did not change significantly before vs after 48 h (Rykkje).\[6\]
- **Field strength:**
  - **Direct comparisons:** no study found compared 1.5 T vs 3 T for benign enhancement.
  - **Indirect evidence:** 3 T studies (Lescher, Bette, Miskin) report earlier reactive enhancement than the 1990s 1.0 T series (Albert, Forsting: "did not occur before day 4").\[24\] That supports, but does not prove, a field-strength effect; methodology also differed.
  - **Confounding:** Rykkje did not stratify by field strength (90.4% were at 1.5 T).\[6\]
- **Surgical factors:**
  - **Ischaemia:** occurs more often after recurrent surgery (80% vs 31%, Gempt). It varies by surgeon (32–72%) and rises with perioperative bleeding (2021 Acta Neurochir).\[38\]\[39\] In a low-grade glioma cohort, intraoperative blood loss ≥300 mL was reportedly associated with infarction (OR 4.62), but this figure could not be matched to the PubMed abstract of the attributed J Neurosurg 2021 paper (Berger et al., 19/82 infarcts), so its source is unconfirmed.
  - **Methaemoglobin:** H2O2 accelerates its formation (in vitro, Meyding-Lamadé).\[3\]\[4\]
  - **Haemostats:** they create their own signal patterns (Learned).
  - **iMRI:** gadolinium given intraoperatively produces enhancement that fades within 72 h (Miskin).\[22\] Lescher found no difference in reactive-enhancement rates between iMRI and non-iMRI groups.\[40\]
  - **Not studied:** no study found measured the effect of 5-ALA, irrigation volume or ventricular opening on early cavity appearance.
- **Tumour features:**
  - **Ischaemia:** larger tumours, temporal location and age are linked to more ischaemia (2021 Acta Neurochir).\[41\]\[42\]
  - **Enhancement:** marginal postsurgical enhancement also develops after resection of preoperatively non-enhancing tumours (Forsyth).\[5\]
  - **Cavity contents:** no study found related necrosis, ventricular contact or preoperative haemorrhage to cavity contents.
- **Net judgement:** for enhancement, timing (and probably field strength) dominates. For DWI changes, surgical and tumour factors dominate. For cavity contents, the evidence is insufficient.

## 8. Guidelines

- **RANO (2010) and RANO 2.0 (2023):**
  - **RANO text:** quoted in trial protocols: enhancement "often develops in the wall of the surgical cavity 48 to 72 hours after surgery… a baseline MRI scan should ideally be obtained within 24 to 48 hours after surgery and no later than 72 hours." DWI is recommended to explain later enhancement from ischaemia.\[25\]
  - **RANO 2.0:** moves the response-assessment baseline for newly diagnosed disease to the post-radiotherapy scan.\[43\]\[44\] The immediate postoperative scan (within 48 h) remains the extent-of-resection reference (secondary reviews; the RANO 2.0 primary paper was not read in this session).
- **EANO 2021 (Weller, Nat Rev Clin Oncol):** recommends 24–48 h. Rykkje notes this rests on a single study (Bette 2016).\[6\]
- **BTIP (Ellingson 2015, 10.1093/neuonc/nov095):** the standardised protocol includes pre- and post-contrast 3D T1, FLAIR, T2 and DWI. I did not re-read the full text in this session, so verify its exact wording on timing and on SWI (to my knowledge, SWI is not mandatory).
- **Neither guideline** gives an explicit rule for labelling blood products. Practice-level guidance (Forsting 1993; BraTS 2024; RANO practical review, Leao 2020 AJNR) relies on comparing pre- and post-contrast T1 or on subtraction.

## 10. Gaps, conflicts and open questions

- **No quantitative signal data:**
  - No study reports cavity-content intensity relative to WM/GM/CSF, or ADC of cavity fluid or clot. The only related numbers are infarct thresholds (the 2021 Acta Neurochir study used ADC <0.7 × 10⁻³ mm²/s) and Melhem's phantom thresholds.\[39\]
  - Your generator's intensity priors must therefore come from your own data.
- **FLAIR-bright, T1-not-dark cavities:** no frequency in the literature. Candidate explanations are blood-tinged CSF or irrigation saline, proteinaceous exudate, fresh clot, and gelatin or haemostat matrix. Ølstad notes that "liquid blood, bloody CSF, and bloody saline exhibit different signals than coagulated blood".\[2\] That this explains your minority is my inference.
- **Conflicts:**
  - Albert/Forsting (1.0 T, 1990s: no benign enhancement before day 4) vs Henegar, Lescher, Bette and Smets (enhancement from 17–23 h or even <2 h).\[2\]\[8\]\[24\]
  - Lescher 15/46 vs 13/46.
  - Rykkje abstract vs table.
  - Smets (80% linear at <2 h) vs Rykkje (60% "no or non-thin" at 0–36 h). These likely reflect different definitions and field strengths.
- **Thick linear and nodular enhancement:** without measurable tumour, these occurred in about 30–40% of GBM scans, and their nature is unresolved without follow-up. Bette classified about 25–38% of linear enhancement as not reactive.\[7\]
- **Not studied:** 5-ALA, irrigation, ventricle opening and haemostat choice as determinants. Paired 1.5 T vs 3 T comparisons for benign enhancement.
- **Datasets:** no public dataset combines ≤72 h timing, a cavity label and explicit blood or thin-linear conventions at scale.
- **Weak evidence:** many numbers are from small single-centre series (Henegar n=11, Forsyth n=17, Smets n=24, Lescher n=46) or abstracts I could not verify in full. Treat them as order-of-magnitude priors.
