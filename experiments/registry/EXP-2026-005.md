## Scientific claim and critical review

- Claim, engineering level: the existing pipeline with its frozen settings turns at least 24 LUMIERE same-week
  pre-op/post-op T1ce pairs into exports that pass the frozen registration rule, with pre-op tumor masks on which two
  independent automatic tools agree.
- Ways the claim fails: the atlas copies are rescaled; too many scans are thick-slice; the RHUH-calibrated registration
  rule rejects most LUMIERE pairs, or accepts wrong pairs; the two tools disagree on the pre-op tumor; fewer than 24
  pairs survive.
- Cheapest falsifiers, in order: the geometry check (text and header files only), the mask comparison, then
  registration.
- The card does not claim: that any LUMIERE scan is within 72 hours of surgery; that any post-op voxel label is
  correct; that LUMIERE improves training (that is run E3 of the plan).
- Measured failure that motivates it: the r2 model is limited by data. Validation image loss was 0.0578 at step 500
  and 0.0580 at step 1000 while training loss kept falling, on 24 accepted RHUH training pairs.
- One change: a second dataset enters the pipeline. Frozen: frame (1 mm, 256 in-plane, built from the pre-op scan
  alone), intensity window (1st-99th percentile of each scan's foreground), rigid registration settings, acceptance
  rule v1, the representation, RHUH's split, every EXP-2026-001 config block.
- Inputs at inference time: pre-op T1ce and a pre-op tumor mask from an automatic tool. Both exist before surgery.
  The post-op scan is a target only. The expert rating (complete or partial resection) is derived from the post-op
  scan: it is recorded for reporting and checks and never enters a prospective input.

Verified sources and their limits:

- According to PubMed, Suter et al., *The LUMIERE dataset*, Sci Data 2022
  ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-022-01881-7)): 91 glioblastoma patients, 638 study dates, one centre, 95%
  Siemens; skull-stripped with HD-BET; time is given in weeks from the pre-op scan; DeepBraTumIA "registers to an
  atlas"; segmentation outliers were left in on purpose; non-commercial use only. The paper does not say whether the
  atlas registration is rigid.
- According to PubMed, Schucht et al., PLoS One 2013 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0079846)): the same
  centre's protocol defines early post-op MRI as within 72 hours. This supports "early by protocol". It does not give
  the day of any released scan.
- According to PubMed, Siakallis and Bisdas, Cancers 2026 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/cancers18203383)): 57
  LUMIERE timepoints in 42 patients were excluded there because the automated masks clearly did not represent tumor.
- Public tables v202211 (Figshare collection 5904905), checked by SHA-256 in `rhuh_synthesis/lumiere.py`. The Figshare
  records state CC0; the paper and readme state non-commercial use. Both are recorded; the stricter one is followed.
- The researcher's earlier notebooks in `notebooks/old_notes/` (saved outputs read, not re-run): DeepBraTumIA label
  IDs, slice thickness of native scans, and the mismatch between DeepBraTumIA's post-op labels and the expert ratings.

Threats:

- Site and scanner: LUMIERE is mostly Siemens; RHUH's post-op scans are GE. Any later training result must report RHUH
  validation on its own.
- Timing: unknown per scan (see below).
- Supervision: pre-op masks are automated and unreviewed; there is no trusted post-op voxel label.
- Preprocessing differences from RHUH: another skull-stripping tool, another atlas, and one more interpolation if the
  atlas copies are used.
- Selection: 8 of 44 same-week pairs have no pre-op mask, and the registration rule removes more. Both filters are
  reported with their denominators.
- Label numbering: LUMIERE's own radiomics table names DeepBraTumIA labels 1 and 2 the other way round.

## Cohort and controls

**Dataset and permissions.** LUMIERE v202211, stored by the researcher at `MyDrive/LUMIERE`. Use is non-commercial
research. Derived files stay private: git-ignored on the laptop or on the researcher's Drive. No patient or study name
goes into a tracked file or a message. Weights trained with LUMIERE data are not released without a separate decision.

**Timing.** The release gives weeks from the pre-op scan, not days from surgery. A same-week post-op scan is 0 to 6
days after the pre-op scan, so at most 6 days after surgery. By the centre's protocol it is an early post-op scan, but
the day is not verified for any scan. In the protocol's strata this cohort is `unknown`, with the note "same week,
early by protocol". It is never counted as a verified 72-hour scan and never joins RHUH's evaluation.

**Labels.**

- Pre-op: DeepBraTumIA's mask, renumbered to RHUH's IDs in memory (DeepBraTumIA 1 enhancing becomes 3, 2 necrotic or
  non-enhancing becomes 1, 3 edema becomes 2). The mapping matched DeepBraTumIA's own volume files in 526 of 526
  studies in the earlier notebooks. Provenance `public`, tool DeepBraTumIA, automated, not reviewed by an expert.
- Post-op: no voxel label is admitted. DeepBraTumIA's post-op mask does not match the expert ratings (after blood
  removal, 14 of 79 complete-resection scans keep more than 1 ml of enhancing label), and its necrosis label is larger
  than the whole pre-op core in 13 of 32 same-week pairs. The only trusted post-op label is the expert rating per
  patient.
- DeepBraTumIA's post-op mask has one use here: it marks a region that the registration metric and the QC leave out,
  as the post-op label does for RHUH. A mask that is too large only removes more voxels from that comparison.
- In the export it is stored as `post_label_unverified`. The key `label`, which the training code reads as the post-op
  target, is absent, so any code that expects a target fails loudly on a LUMIERE export.

**Sequences and geometry.** Only T1ce is exported. Native scans are skull-stripped and unregistered; native T1ce is
mostly 1 mm 3D, with a few 6-mm scans (3 of 58 sampled earlier). DeepBraTumIA's atlas copies are 182 x 218 x 182 at
1 mm in one orientation; their transforms are linear in 60 of 60 sampled studies. Whether they rescale the head is
not yet checked.

**Steps.** Each writes a write-once record with its hash. Sources are only read.

1. **Files.** Revised 2026-11-21 after the researcher's question. The full run is on Colab against Drive, in one
   notebook, with outputs through the Drive API. Every step is CPU work (the registration is SimpleITK on the CPU), so
   a GPU runtime is not needed and brings no speed-up by itself. Four pairs (3 complete and 1 partial resection, drawn
   with seed 2026) are copied to the laptop so the code is developed and tested on real files before that run. These
   four patients are pinned to the training role, as RHUH's development cases were. Files per study: native `CT1`,
   the four atlas copies, DeepBraTumIA's atlas and native masks, its transform and volume files, and HD-GLIO-AUTO's
   native mask.
2. **Manifest.** One entry per pair: paths, file and voxel hashes, geometry of the native and atlas T1ce, border
   background, tool versions where recorded, expert rating, timing note, licence note. Exact duplicate volumes are
   reported.
3. **Geometry check.** For the 72 studies of the eligible pairs: the singular values of the matrix in `ct1.tfm`, and
   the voxel size of native T1ce.
   - Route rule: if every singular value is within 0.001 of 1, the atlas copies are rigid and are used (uniform 1 mm,
     same grid on both sides, head pose normalized as in RHUH). If not, the card stops here with the measured scales,
     and the researcher chooses between the native scans and atlas copies with the scaling removed.
   - Thick-slice rule: a pair is excluded if either native T1ce has a voxel side above 2 mm. This holds on either
     route, because an atlas copy of a thick-slice scan is only interpolated. Two reasons. A scan with 5 or 6 mm
     slices holds one measured slice in five or six; the rest of a 1-mm copy is interpolation, and residual tumor is
     about 5 mm thick. And the limit keeps LUMIERE inside RHUH's own range: in RHUH's DICOM headers (one header per
     series, read 2026-11-21) the T1ce series are 3D with slices of 2 mm or less in 79 of the 80 pre-op and post-op
     scans (40 of 40 pre-op, 39 of 40 post-op; the other one is a 2D scan at 5 mm). T1ce was told from T1 by the
     series name in 37 of 40 patients per visit and by being the only 3D T1 series in 3.
   - **Result (2026-11-21, Colab run on commit 35d9ec4, all 72 studies read, none failed):** every `ct1.tfm` holds
     one linear transform, and all 72 have every singular value within 0.001 of 1 (all equal 1.00000 at five
     decimals; determinant 1.00000). The route rule therefore selects the **atlas copies**: 182 x 218 x 182 at 1 mm,
     LAS, the same grid for all 72 studies. Native T1ce: 68 studies have no voxel side above 1.05 mm and 4 studies (in
     3 pairs) have a side above 2 mm; none lies in between, so any limit from 1.05 to 2 mm gives the same result. The
     thick-slice rule excludes those **3 pairs, leaving 33**. Their split by expert rating was not printed.
   - **Second check on the 4 sample pairs (8 studies, on the laptop):** DeepBraTumIA's mask moved from native to
     atlas space with the inverse of the stored transform matches its atlas mask with Dice 0.993 to 0.999, and label
     volumes agree within 0.03 ml; without the transform, Dice is 0.00 to 0.78. Native and atlas T1ce correlate 0.93
     to 0.99 after the transform. So the atlas copies are rigid resamplings of the native scans, and mm and ml are
     preserved. The stored transform maps native points to atlas points.
   - Observation, not a rule: brain volume in the atlas copies differs from the released native scans by -2% to +10%
     in the 8 sample studies. The atlas copies carry DeepBraTumIA's own skull stripping; the released native scans
     carry HD-BET's. Which brain mask defines the foreground of an export is decided before step 7 and recorded.
4. **Pre-op mask check.** Revised 2026-11-21 for the atlas route: the comparison is made in atlas space. HD-GLIO-AUTO's
   native mask lies on the native T1ce grid (8 of 8 sample studies), so DeepBraTumIA's CT1 transform moves it into
   atlas space, where it is compared with DeepBraTumIA's atlas mask. HD-GLIO-AUTO's label IDs (1 and 2 in the sample)
   are first confirmed from the atlas images (the enhancing label has the higher T1ce-minus-T1 signal), as was done
   for DeepBraTumIA. DeepBraTumIA's native masks cover the same physical box as native T1ce but are stored with the
   axes in another order in 6 of 8 sample studies; they are not needed on the atlas route.
   - **Found 2026-11-21 during implementation, needs the researcher's confirmation before the full run:** in all 8
     sample studies, HD-GLIO-AUTO's native mask as stored lies off the tumor. Its overlap with DeepBraTumIA's mask is
     Dice 0.00, and up to 77% of it falls outside the brain. Flipping its voxel array along index axes 0 and 2,
     keeping its header, puts it on the tumor: whole-lesion Dice with DeepBraTumIA 0.54 to 0.90. The native T1ce
     alone, without DeepBraTumIA, confirms this: robust z of T1ce in its brightest label is 0.98 to 2.50 when
     flipped (DeepBraTumIA's enhancing label: 0.99 to 2.50) against -0.30 to 0.50 as stored, with 97% to 100% of the
     flipped lesion inside the brain. Mirroring through the world origin (a left-right and front-back convention
     error) does not fit. The released files appear to be stored flipped; why is not known.
   - Rule added for this: one reading of all HD-GLIO-AUTO masks is chosen from the pre-op studies by image evidence
     alone. The candidates are as stored, flipped along index axes 0 and 2, or flipped along the two axes that carry
     the world's left-right and front-back directions. These two flips coincide for the sample's orientation. Each
     study votes for the reading that puts its brightest label highest on native T1ce. The run stops unless one
     reading gets at least 90% of the votes. No study is read differently from the others.
   - **Revised 2026-11-22 for run r2, after run r1 and its reading check. This is a deviation found after r1.** The
     reading is chosen per group of studies that share a native orientation.
     - The orientation is the axis codes of the HD-GLIO-AUTO header. It equals the T1ce header in all 66 studies.
     - Within each group the pre-op studies vote as before. The run stops unless one reading gets at least 90% of
       that group's votes.
     - A post-op study is read the way its orientation group is read. It does not vote: after a resection its
       labels are not reliably bright. A post-op study whose orientation has no pre-op group is not read, and its
       numbers are reported as missing.
     - Why: in r1 the votes split exactly by orientation. All 30 PIR studies voted for the flipped files and all 3
       LPS studies for the stored files. r1's rule excluded the 3 LPS pairs although the tools agree on them (Dice
       0.73 to 0.89).
     - Unchanged: the three candidate readings; the evidence (native T1ce only, so DeepBraTumIA plays no part in a
       vote); the 90% level; the 0.5 flag rule; both controls; the split rule and seed; the pinned patients; the
       registration and rule v1.
     - r2 replaces r1's mask check, split and exports before anything has used them. Nothing has been trained,
       tuned or scored on r1's pairs. Only r1's count summary was read; it includes registration QC spreads over
       all 28 exported pairs. r1's records stay on Drive.
     - The development set is redrawn by the same rule and seed from the larger set of pairs, so its members may
       differ from r1's.
     - Config: `configs/experiments/EXP-2026-005_r2.json`. r1's config file is not edited.
   - Flag rule: a pair is excluded if the Dice between the two tools' pre-op enhancing labels is below 0.5. The rule
     is meant to catch gross failures of one tool, not to grade masks.
   - Reported without a rule: enhancing volume ratio, distance between the two whole-lesion centroids, pre-op core
     volume, and the same numbers for the post-op masks for information.
   - Control: the comparison is also run with the pre-op masks of other patients (a seeded shuffle). It must flag
     them; if it does not, the check has no power on atlas-aligned data and is reported as such.
5. **Split.** Before registration. Patient level, seed 2026, stratified by the expert rating, 80% training and 20%
   development by largest remainder. For 36 pairs this gives 29 training (22 complete, 7 partial) and 7 development
   (5 and 2); after the 3 thick-slice exclusions the counts change and are recomputed. Pairs excluded in steps 3 and 4
   are removed first and counted. There is no LUMIERE test set: RHUH is the only evaluation cohort for claims.
   - Open, raised by the researcher on 2026-11-21: put all LUMIERE pairs into training and validate on RHUH alone.
     What is fixed either way: RHUH's roles are frozen and are not reshuffled (its test set is sealed, and its
     training patients have been seen by the r2 model and by the exploratory analyses); in training, RHUH and LUMIERE
     pairs are mixed in the same batches. What the choice trades: 7 more training pairs (36 instead of 29, about 12%
     of the pooled training set) against held-out patients (RHUH validation has 3 accepted patients; with the LUMIERE
     development set there are 10). A LUMIERE development patient is scored on the image against the real post-op
     scan, which needs no post-op mask. Limit: once LUMIERE pairs are in training (run E3), a gain on LUMIERE
     development patients is partly a same-site gain and does not by itself show a gain on RHUH.
6. **Registration.** `data.prepare_patient` unchanged, with the frozen settings: rigid, post-op to the pre-op frame,
   lesion regions left out of the metric. Acceptance is the frozen rule v1 applied automatically in both roles: far
   correlation at least 0.5 and outline Dice at least 0.93. A flagged pair is quarantined and stays in the
   denominator. Nobody rescues a pair by looking at it.
   - Control: each training-role post-op scan is also registered to the pre-op scan of another patient (a seeded
     shuffle). Rule v1 should reject these. If it accepts many, it cannot tell a good registration from a bad one on
     this data, and that is reported before anything else is concluded.
   - The researcher looks at the overlays of training-role pairs to spot systematic problems, not to change decisions.
7. **Export.** RHUH's export format in the 1-mm pre-op frame, with `post_label_unverified` instead of `label` and a
   sidecar that records the route, the rating, the timing note and label provenance. Also reported: the share of the
   pre-op foreground and of the pre-op labels that falls inside the frozen 192-mm crop.
   - The representation gate is not rerun. It tests post-op mask targets through the VAE, and LUMIERE has none here.

**Seeds, budget, hardware.** Split seed 2026; registration sampling seed 2026 (frozen). CPU only. No GPU and no model
in this card.

## Frozen evaluation before final test

This card has no model and no test set. Its gate measures are:

- **Primary:** number of accepted pairs under rule v1, out of the 36 eligible, by role and by expert rating.
- **Gate A:** at least 24 accepted pairs in total. This is the size of RHUH's accepted training set. For reference
  (recounted 2026-11-17 from the RHUH QC tables): the rule alone passed 23 of 28 RHUH training pairs and 3 of 6
  validation pairs; a 24th training pair was accepted after overlay review, which LUMIERE pairs do not get. At the
  same pass rate about 28 of 36 LUMIERE pairs would pass, but that rate was measured on another dataset.
- **Secondary:** transform scales and the route taken; pairs excluded as thick-slice; mask agreement and pairs
  flagged; both shuffle controls; label volumes and 3D components before and after resampling; displacement; crop
  coverage; pre-op to post-op intensity correlation far from the lesion.
- **Statistics:** counts with denominators. No test is needed. Patients are the unit everywhere.
- **Overlay looks by the researcher:** training-role pairs only; a technical look with no clinical meaning.

Stop conditions:

- The public tables on disk do not match v202211, or a pair file is missing or unreadable.
- The atlas transforms are not rigid (step 3): stop for the route decision.
- A shuffle control passes for more than a quarter of the shuffled pairs: stop and report before going on.

Decision rules:

- `PROMOTE`: at least 24 accepted pairs and both controls behave. The accepted training-role pairs may be used by
  later cards (scaffold check C4, run E3); development pairs stay held out.
- `ITERATE`: fewer than 24 only because rule v1 rejects pairs that the overlays show to be well aligned. A LUMIERE
  rule may then be proposed as a new policy version, calibrated on training-role pairs only, in a card revision.
- `BLOCKED`: fewer than 24 for any other reason. LUMIERE pairs are then not used as paired training data.
  - Fallback raised by the researcher on 2026-11-21: use LUMIERE's post-op scans without pairing, to teach post-op
    appearance. The protocol allows unpaired scans for distribution learning. The smallest form needs no new stream:
    LUMIERE post-op slices are trained with the pre-op condition channels switched off. A separate reference-image
    stream, fed like the text stream, is the retrieval approach on the baseline ladder (Kang et al.) and a larger
    change. Either one needs its own card; neither is designed or tested here.
- `REJECT`: a leak or a geometry error that cannot be repaired.
