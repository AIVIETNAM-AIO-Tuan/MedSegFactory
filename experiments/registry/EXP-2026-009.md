## Scientific claim and critical review

- **Claim.** A generator that outputs pre-contrast T1 together with T1ce can show two cases with labels to match:
  - material bright on both sequences, which is blood-like and not residual tumour;
  - a rim bright on T1ce only, which is enhancement.
  A T1ce-only generator cannot show this difference, so a segmenter trained on its output cannot learn it.
- **Why it matters.** Sources and exact numbers are in `docs/CITATION_LEDGER.md`.
  - Early scans often hold blood: intradural blood on 63% of scans at 12–48 h (Johnstad 2024).
  - Blood confuses the reading: methaemoglobin interfered with residual assessment in 6 of 17 patients (Forsyth
    1997), and 24 of 54 patients were T1-bright soon after surgery (Meyding-Lamadé 1993).
  - The consensus protocol requires pre- and post-contrast T1 with matched parameters (Ellingson 2015).
  - In our data:
    - the scaffold's losses sit in cores that are not dark on T1 (C4 follow-up 2);
    - two RHUH patients without residual tumour have a T1-bright wall (C4 follow-up 3).
- **Null and alternatives:**
  - the generated T1 is a blurred copy of the generated T1ce and carries no information of its own;
  - the model learns the T1 targets' blur;
  - a downstream segmenter ignores the T1 channel.
- **Data constraints, verified:**
  - RHUH: T1 is a 2D fast spin echo with 5-mm slices, while T1ce is 3D at 1 mm (RHUH data paper, Table 3). The
    processed T1 is resampled to 1 mm, so its detail across slices stays limited.
  - LUMIERE: native T1 is mostly 5–6 mm. The DeepBraTumIA atlas copies are resampled to 1 mm.
  - So a generated T1 can at best match these targets, and a thin rim (1–2 mm) is blurred on T1 in the targets
    themselves. The thick T1 also limits the "enhancement" measure of C4 follow-ups 2 and 3.
- **What stays frozen:** the training recipe, the evaluation regions, the splits, and E-eval for the T1ce output.
- **Inputs at inference:**
  - Pre-op T1ce, pre-op T1, the pre-op mask and the supplied scenario. RHUH and LUMIERE have pre-op T1 for every
    pair used here.
  - Outputs: post-op T1ce, post-op T1 and the mask.

## Design options (for decision)

- **A. Pack T1 into the image stream's channels.**
  - The image stream takes (T1ce, T1, T1ce) as its 3 channels in place of (T1ce, T1ce, T1ce). The decoded channels
    are read back separately.
  - No change to the architecture.
  - Risk: the Stable Diffusion VAE was trained on natural RGB images, whose channels are correlated, so decoupled
    channels may reconstruct poorly.
- **B.** A separate T1 image stream: a change to the architecture, with more parameters to learn from 49 pairs.
- **C.** Two stages, with a separate T1 generator conditioned on the generated T1ce: twice the training.
- **Recommendation: A first, gated by its cheapest falsifier.**
  - The falsifier is a VAE round trip of packed images on RHUH training slices.
  - Compare per-channel error and SSIM against single-channel round trips of the same slices.
  - The pass threshold is fixed before the check; for example, the packed T1 channel's MAE is at most 1.5 times
    its single-channel MAE.
  - The check uses no training: a short notebook, run by the researcher.

## Evaluation (proposed, frozen before any run)

- **T1ce output:** E-eval unchanged. The T1 channel must not make T1ce worse beyond the E0s noise floor.
- **T1 output:** primary-region MAE against the real post-op T1, per patient, with the same regions as E-eval.
- **The key test:**
  - On held-out pairs, compare the generated T1's core and shell signal with the real one, by the follow-up 2
    reading (z against far tissue).
  - The question: when the scenario asks for a cavity that is not dark, does the generated T1 become bright where
    T1ce is bright?
  - This needs the cavity-content scenario element (EXP-2026-007, inputs from the literature check).
- **Downstream, later:** rET segmentation with T1 and T1ce as inputs, reading false positives on T1-bright
  cavities.

## Cohort and controls

- Training: RHUH's 24 accepted training patients and LUMIERE's 25 accepted training-role pairs, each with T1.
- Held-out: as in E-eval.
- Controls:
  - the T1ce-only model (E0);
  - a T1 channel filled with T1ce (the packing alone, no new information);

about the thick-slice T1, and raised making the model 2.5D.

- Notes for decision 3 (Claude, 2026-11-25; reasoning, nothing measured):
  - 2.5D cannot add detail across slices to the T1 targets: on the 1-mm grid, adjacent T1 slices are interpolated
    from the same 5-mm acquired slices. Decision 3 is needed either way.
  - 2.5D answers another question: whether slices generated one at a time agree with each other. No disagreement
    has been measured yet. The localized sampler starts from the scaffold, which is consistent in 3D, and D2 found
    that the start dominated the output at the residual pieces.
  - Packing adjacent slices into the VAE's 3 channels would compete with option A for those channels. Adding the
    neighbours' latents to the UNet input, as the pre-op condition is added now (`model.py`), changes the input
    layer and needs training. Context from pre-op neighbours alone does not make the generated slices agree.
  - A readout of differences between adjacent generated slices in E-eval, which would measure that agreement at no
    GPU cost, was proposed on 2026-11-25 and postponed by the researcher.

## Step 1: the VAE round trip of option A (frozen 2026-11-27, before the run)

- Started by the researcher on 2026-11-27 ("được, làm đi"), after E round 1, as decision 1 says.
- Cohort: RHUH's 24 accepted training patients, post-op visit. No validation or test patient is read.
- Images:
  - T1ce is the export's post-op image.
  - T1 is the same visit's processed file, moved into the export frame with the stored rigid matrix
    (`core_signal.sequences_in_frame`). The moved T1ce must reproduce the export within 1e-6, or the run stops.
  - T1 is normalized as the export normalized T1ce: the 1st–99th percentile window of the native file inside its
    foreground, applied inside the moved foreground and the post-op support.
  - Slices: the W4 generation slab (every axial slice that touches `E`), on the model's working grid (crop 192 mm,
    512 px).
- Round trips with the frozen Stable Diffusion 1.5 VAE (posterior mean, no sampling, float32):
  - single T1ce and single T1: the gray slice repeated to the 3 channels and decoded as the pipeline does
    (channels clamped and averaged);
  - packed: (T1ce, T1, T1ce); T1ce is read back as the mean of channels 0 and 2, and T1 as channel 1.
- Scores: MAE inside the brain of each slice, averaged over the patient's slices. SSIM (`evaluation.roi_ssim`) is
  descriptive.
- **Rule:** option A passes if, for T1 and for T1ce, the median over the 24 patients of packed MAE / single MAE is
  at most 1.5. This is the card's example value, kept unchanged. Otherwise option A fails, and B or C is considered
  (decision 2).
- Also reported: per-patient ratios (numbered, without names), their range, and how many patients exceed 1.5.
- Code: `rhuh_synthesis/vae_packing.py`. It is run in a Colab cell by the researcher; no training.
- Checks before the run (local, CPU), done after the rule above was written:
  - 4 unit tests with stub VAEs: channel routing, a perfect round trip, the decision rule;
  - the data path on one training patient: the moved T1ce reproduces the export;
  - the real VAE on 2 of that patient's slices, only to exercise the code. Its numbers are not a result and change
    nothing above;
  - a dry run of the Colab cell on 2 training patients with a stub VAE.

## Step 2: a tissue-class map from the post-op sequences (option D; frozen 2026-11-27, before the run)

- Started by the researcher on 2026-11-27 ("thử bản đồ lớp mô đi"), after option A failed.
- Idea:
  - The cross-sequence relations go into one class map, not into extra image streams.
  - The map would become a training target of the existing mask stream; the unused post-op ID-1 channel is the
    candidate.
  - T1 for a downstream segmenter could then be rendered by rule from the map and the pre-op T1.
  - This step only builds the map and tests it. No training, no model.
- Cohort: RHUH's 24 accepted training patients, post-op visit (the same as C4 follow-up 2). No validation or test
  patient.
- Region `S`:
  - the pre-op core `K` (IDs 1 and 3), dilated by 5 mm;
  - inside the primary region restricted to the post-op support, as in C4;
  - inside every sequence's foreground.
  - Why 5 mm: in the exploratory mask check of 2026-11-27, the real residual lay inside the core or within 5 mm of
    it.
- Classes: `core_signal.classify` applied voxel by voxel, unchanged (`RULE` as frozen for C4 follow-up 2).
  - Inputs: robust z of post-op T1, T1ce and T2 against far normal tissue (`S_p`), and enhancement
    = z(T1ce) − z(T1).
  - Codes: 1 enhancing, 2 t1_bright (blood-like), 3 dark on T1 and T2, 4 fluid-like (dark T1, bright T2),
    5 tissue-like, 6 mixed.
  - No smoothing or component filter in this version.
- Readouts, per patient, then medians:
  - volume of each class in `S`, in ml;
  - the excess of "enhancing" and "t1_bright" in `S` over their share in far normal tissue;
  - where RHUH's expert post-op label holds enhancing tumour (ID 3) inside `S`:
    - recall: the share of expert ID 3 voxels coded enhancing;
    - conflict: the share coded t1_bright;
    - precision: the share of the enhancing code that lies on expert ID 3;
  - in patients without expert ID 3: the volume coded enhancing (benign enhancement, vessels or noise).
- **Rule:**
  - **GO** to a training-target card needs all three:
    - median recall at least 0.5 over the patients with expert ID 3;
    - median conflict at most 0.2;
    - the researcher's review of the pages judges the blood-like class plausible in at least half of the patients
      with 0.5 ml of it or more.
  - If any condition fails: **ITERATE**, with a new rule version built on training patients only, or **STOP**.
  - Precision is reported, not gated: benign wall enhancement is common early (see `docs/CITATION_LEDGER.md`), and
    the expert label counts tumour only.
- Review pages (private, numbered without names), for the researcher; no clinician is available. For each patient,
  at the slice with the most `S`:
  - post-op T1ce, T1 and T2;
  - the class map over T1ce;
  - the expert ID-3 outline.
- Found in the synthetic tests, before the run:
  - The rule is voxelwise and unsmoothed, and it checks "enhancing" first.
  - The difference of two noisy z maps passes the enhancing threshold by chance in some voxels: about 12% of planted
    blood voxels, and a background rate in normal tissue.
  - So precision is low by construction, and the excess over far tissue is the fairer reading.
  - The gated conflict (expert ID 3 coded t1_bright) is not affected in this direction.
- Known limits:
  - RHUH's T1 has 5-mm slices, so thin rims are partial-volume blurred;
  - the classes describe signal, not tissue;
  - the expert label is the only reference, and it covers enhancing tumour only.

## Run evidence and decision (complete after experiments)

- Step 1 (2026-11-27, Colab GPU, commit baef64b, run by the researcher). The notebook with its outputs is kept
  privately in `data/RHUH-GBM/synthesis/EXP-2026-009/`.
  - 24 of 24 patients ran (27 to 66 slices each); every moved T1ce reproduced its export.
  - **Decision by the frozen rule: FAIL.**
    - T1 passes: median packed/single MAE ratio 1.05 (range 0.82 to 1.29; no patient above 1.5). Median MAE 0.0187
      single, 0.0191 packed.
    - T1ce fails: median ratio 1.80 (range 1.32 to 2.38; 22 of 24 patients above 1.5). Median MAE 0.0130 single,
      0.0232 packed. SSIM falls from about 0.96 to about 0.87.
  - So option A, as frozen, would roughly double the T1ce reconstruction error that E-eval's output already
    carries. Option A is not taken. Decision 2 now asks for B or C.
- Exploratory probe after the result (post hoc, CPU, 2 training patients × 3 slices; not a result):
  - Probe 1, single T1ce round trip: the per-channel MAE was R 0.011, G 0.015, B 0.013. The green channel is not
    the most faithful, so the failure is not a "luminance channel" effect.
  - Probe 2, swapped order (T1, T1ce, T1): T1ce read from G got worse (0.036, against 0.025 in the frozen order),
    while T1 stayed near 0.020. So no channel order rescues T1ce.
  - Candidate reading (unverified): the VAE keeps detail that differs between channels poorly. T1 has little detail
    of its own: it was acquired with 5-mm slices. T1ce has 1-mm detail, which packing blurs.
- Step 2, tissue-class map v1 (2026-11-27, laptop CPU, code 7646176; private record
  `data/RHUH-GBM/synthesis/EXP-2026-009/tissue_classes_v1/tissue_classes_v1.json`, canonical SHA-256 42518c6ba679…,
  with 24 numbered review pages):
  - 24 of 24 patients mapped, none NA; 7 hold expert ID 3 inside `S`.
  - Automatic conditions: both pass.
    - Median recall 0.72 (≥ 0.5): the enhancing code covers most of the expert's residual tumour.
    - Median conflict 0.006 (≤ 0.2).
    - Median precision 0.11, which is not gated.
  - The review condition is pending: 16 patients hold at least 0.5 ml of the blood-like code.
  - Readings the rule did not anticipate:
    - The blood-like code is not specific.
      - Its share in `S` is below its share in far normal tissue: excess median −0.066, with 5 of 24 patients
        positive.
      - z(T1) ≥ 1 against the whole far brain is likely reached by normal white matter. This is a candidate reading,
        and the pages can show it.
    - The enhancing code sits near its far-tissue background: excess median 0.000 (IQR −0.018 to +0.013). In
      patients without expert ID 3 it covers a median of 2.4 ml, so as a map it is mostly vessel- or noise-level.
    - "mixed" is the largest code (median 29 ml), and fluid-like is next (median 13 ml).
  - So the automatic gate passes, but the numbers already say the blood-like class needs a stricter rule before it
    can be a training target. The researcher's page review decides between GO and ITERATE.
  - The researcher's review, started on page 11 (a patient without expert ID 3): the enhancing code follows the
    interhemispheric fissure and the cortical surface, and several areas are ambiguous.
  - Diagnostics after that review (post hoc, same 24 patients; the script and values are private beside the record):
    - The enhancing code is mostly thin, vessel-like structure.
      - A one-voxel morphological opening removes 64% of it in the 17 patients without expert ID 3 (median 2.37 ml
        down to 0.86 ml).
      - The same opening lowers the expert recall from 0.72 to 0.64 and raises precision only from 0.11 to 0.15.
    - Excluding a 3-mm band at the brain surface changes almost nothing: 13% of the enhancing code lies there,
      against 6% of `S`. The fissure lies inside the brain mask.
    - The blood-like code sits at normal tissue brightness.
      - Its voxels have a median z(T1) of 1.24, while 10% of far normal tissue has z(T1) >= 1 and the far 99th
        percentile is 1.55.
      - Above that percentile, a median of 0.23 ml remains per patient, and 10 of 24 patients keep at least 0.5 ml.
    - Reading: v1's enhancing and blood-like codes mostly mark normal anatomy (vessels, white matter). Signal alone
      does not separate residual tumour from vessels or benign enhancement; precision stays near 0.15.
  - **Decision for v1: ITERATE.** The review and the diagnostics agree. v1 is not used as a training target.
